# [Apollo](https://www.apollo.io/?via=outreachly) vs Acuity for Freelancers: Which Scheduling Platform is Right for Your Coaching Business?

Managing client appointments while building your freelance coaching or consulting business can feel like juggling flaming torches. You're trying to focus on delivering great results for your clients, but you're also drowning in scheduling emails, calendar conflicts, and administrative overhead.

The good news? There are powerful scheduling tools designed specifically for coaches and consultants who want to streamline their booking process, reduce no-shows, and spend less time managing calendars and more time serving clients.

If you've been researching scheduling solutions, you've probably stumbled across Apollo and Acuity. Both are popular choices among solo entrepreneurs and small business owners. But here's the thing: they take fundamentally different approaches to solving the scheduling problem.

This guide breaks down everything you need to know about Apollo versus Acuity so you can make an informed decision for your business.

## Understanding the Two Solutions

Before diving into comparisons, let's clarify what each platform actually does.

### What is Apollo?

Apollo is a sales intelligence and lead database platform that includes scheduling capabilities. It's primarily designed for sales professionals and B2B companies who need to research prospects, manage contacts, and coordinate meetings with potential clients.

When we talk about Apollo's scheduling features, we're referring to its calendar integration and meeting scheduling tools that help sales teams manage outreach and demos. The platform excels at prospecting and lead management but treats scheduling as a supporting feature rather than its core purpose.

### What is Acuity Scheduling?

Acuity Scheduling (now part of Squarespace) is a dedicated appointment booking and scheduling platform built specifically for service-based businesses. It's used by coaches, consultants, therapists, fitness trainers, and other professionals who sell their time and expertise.

Acuity is purpose-built for client-facing scheduling, which means the platform's entire architecture revolves around making it easy for clients to book appointments with you while minimizing your administrative burden.

## Scheduling Features and User Experience

### Apollo's Scheduling Approach

Apollo includes scheduling functionality primarily to support its sales workflow. When you use Apollo, you're getting a scheduling feature that's integrated within a larger prospecting and CRM platform.

The scheduling tools in Apollo help you:
- Send meeting invitations to prospects
- Coordinate calendar availability
- Track meeting outcomes
- Integrate with your existing email workflow

However, Apollo doesn't provide public booking pages in the way that dedicated scheduling platforms do. Instead, you're manually managing invitations and coordinating availability with potential clients through the platform.

For a coach or consultant running a small practice, this means you'll still need to handle a lot of back-and-forth communication about scheduling. Your clients can't simply click a link and book available times with you—you're doing more of the coordination work.

### Acuity's Scheduling Approach

Acuity is built from the ground up as a scheduling platform. Its core strength is making it dead simple for your clients to book appointments with you.

Here's how it works in practice:
1. You define your availability and service offerings in Acuity
2. You get a unique booking link or embedded booking widget
3. You share that link with clients or embed it on your website
4. Clients see your availability and book appointments instantly
5. Automatic confirmations, reminders, and follow-ups are sent to both parties

This eliminates the email ping-pong that typically surrounds scheduling. A new potential client can visit your website, see your availability, and book a consultation without ever sending you an email about scheduling.

The user experience is night and day different. With Acuity, you're creating a frictionless booking experience. With Apollo's scheduling features, you're still managing the back-and-forth coordination manually.

## Payment Processing and Client Management

One of the biggest pain points for freelance coaches and consultants is collecting payments. Many platforms force you to use separate tools for scheduling and payment processing, which creates friction and reduces conversion rates.

### Apollo's Payment Capabilities

Apollo doesn't include native payment processing for service fees. If you want to collect payment through Apollo, you'd need to integrate a separate payment processor and manually invoice clients.

This means your booking workflow might look like:
1. Coordinate schedule with client in Apollo
2. Send invoice via email or separate invoice tool
3. Wait for client payment
4. Confirm appointment

It's workable, but it's not streamlined.

### Acuity's Payment Capabilities

Acuity has payment processing built directly into the platform. You can collect payment when clients book their appointments, eliminating uncertainty about whether clients will actually pay for their sessions.

Acuity supports:
- Credit card payments via Stripe or Square
- PayPal payments
- Invoicing for payments after the appointment
- Payment plans for packages and retainers
- Deposits and installments

For a coach running a consulting practice, this is a massive advantage. You can require payment upfront when clients book, which dramatically reduces no-shows and creates a more professional client experience.

## Customization and Branding

Your booking page and client-facing experience are extensions of your brand. How much control do these platforms give you?

### Apollo's Customization

As a sales-focused platform, Apollo's customization around scheduling is limited. You can add some basic branding, but the overall experience feels like you're directing people into a sales system rather than a premium service offering.

This might be fine for internal team coordination, but it's not ideal for a consultant trying to convey professionalism and expertise to potential clients.

### Acuity's Customization

Acuity shines when it comes to customization and branding. You get:
- Custom colors and fonts matching your brand
- Ability to add your logo and branding
- Custom background images
- Customizable question fields for client intake
- Custom messages and confirmation emails
- Mobile-responsive design that looks professional on any device

Many successful coaches use Acuity as a core part of their client-facing experience. The booking page feels integrated with their brand rather than like a third-party tool.

## Pricing Comparison

Budget matters, especially for freelancers just starting out.

### Apollo's Pricing

Apollo requires contacting their sales team for pricing. Typically, Apollo plans start around $49/month for basic access but can go significantly higher depending on features and data access. There's often a minimum commitment required.

For a freelancer just starting out, Apollo's pricing structure makes it less accessible. You might be paying for enterprise-level features you don't need.

### Acuity's Pricing

Acuity uses straightforward, transparent pricing:
- **Growing Plan**: $15/month - Perfect for getting started
- **Professional Plan**: $25/month - Standard for active coaches and consultants
- **Premier Plan**: $55/month - For high-volume practitioners

Acuity also offers a 7-day free trial with no credit card required, so you can test it thoroughly before committing.

For most solo coaches and consultants, the Professional Plan at $25/month covers everything you need.

- Require advanced prospecting and research capabilities
- Are selling B2B services and managing complex sales cycles
- Already use Apollo for sales prospecting and want scheduling integrated

### Choose Acuity If You:

## Making Your Decision: Key Questions to Ask Yourself

Before choosing between these platforms, ask yourself:

**1. Is scheduling my primary need?**
If yes, Acuity. If you need broader sales intelligence and prospecting, Apollo might make sense alongside another dedicated scheduling tool.

**2. How much do I want clients managing their own bookings?**
Acuity excels at self-service booking. Apollo requires more manual coordination. If you want to minimize scheduling communication, Acuity wins.

**3. Do I want to collect payment at booking time?**
If yes, Acuity's built-in payment processing is a major advantage. Apollo would require integration with another payment system.

**4. What's my budget and complexity tolerance?**
Acuity is transparent on pricing and easier to implement. Apollo requires sales conversations and typically costs more for freelancers who don't need its full feature set.

**5. What does my current tech stack look like?**
Consider which integrations matter most for your workflow. Both platforms integrate well with common business tools, but Acuity's integrations are tailored to service-based businesses.
